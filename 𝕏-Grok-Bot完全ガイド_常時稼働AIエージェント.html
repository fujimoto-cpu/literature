📝 投稿内容

Grok Bot: The Complete and Updated Guide (August 2026) The most powerful agent software on the market… if you can afford it

Elon and the SpaceXAI team (together with Cursor) have officially done it. Grok Bot is, as of today, the most powerful and plug-and-play AI agent available. I have ruthlessly tested Grok Bot since its early beta launch (August 11, 2026) and I can confirm: it is the most useful agent tool on the market right now. It is not a chatbot. It is a team of AI teammates that work 24/7 on their own cloud computer.

I. What exactly is Grok Bot? Grok Bot is SpaceXAI’s (formerly xAI) always-on agent product, developed in collaboration with Cursor. Each Bot is an AI teammate that gets its own persistent cloud computer (a Linux virtual machine with Chrome browser, file system, terminal, roughly 128 GB of storage and 16 GB of RAM). It can sign in to your tools (Gmail, Slack, LinkedIn, Salesforce, Zendesk, Google Sheets, etc.), work exactly the way you would, and return with finished work. It only interrupts you when it needs approval. Key difference from a normal chat: a chat session dies the moment you close the window. A Grok Bot keeps working even after you shut your laptop. How the “swarm” works: you create multiple Bots, each with a role (research, outreach, finance, etc.). They share one computer (isolation is at the user-account level, not per Bot), can coordinate with each other, pass work around, and maintain shared context memory.

Grok Bot vs OpenClaw / Hermes Agent: OpenClaw and Hermes are genuinely powerful and open-source, but require configuring your own always-on machine (VPS or local), keeping skill files updated, cleaning context, and managing infrastructure. Grok Bot is completely plug-and-play: download the app, log in, and you’re done. Trade-offs: total convenience vs data sovereignty (closed and cloud-hosted); models optimized for Grok (4.5/4.6); high price and bundled (not sold standalone).

II. How to set it up and use it (step-by-step) Prerequisites: an eligible plan — SuperGrok Heavy (300/month),CursorUltra(200/month), or Cursor Premium Teams ($120/seat/month) — plus the desktop app (macOS/Windows) or iOS app. Setup: confirm/upgrade plan → download and install the app (x.ai/bot or cursor.com/bot/onboarding) → sign in → create your first Bot (name, primary job, description) → give it a first task (outcome + sources + constraints + deliverable + review point) → for authenticated tools, request “takeover” to enter credentials/2FA once (sessions persist) → install plugins/connectors from Settings.

III. Real workflow examples and prompts Custom or predefined roles (Sales Outbound, Talent Scout, Expense Manager, Chief of Staff, etc.). Examples from the author’s team:

  • Alex – Research Assistant: scans top YouTube channels daily, finds outliers by views/subscribers ratio, delivers a 5-bullet brief + spin-off ideas.
  • Sandra – Real Estate Scout: checks listings twice daily, flags anything 25%+ under market value (found villas 26-37% under market).
  • Jonathan – CFO: reads portfolio data via API, gives daily updates on overnight moves and positions needing attention.
  • Lucy – Executive Assistant: lives in Slack, does morning triage of what’s actually urgent.
  • Oscar – Generalist: general assistant maintaining context of preferences/projects.

IV. Honest analysis and key takeaways Is it worth it? Pound for pound, yes — it removes the setup/maintenance headache of OpenClaw/Hermes. The biggest bottleneck is pricing: not sold standalone, bundled at 200−300/month(or120/seat for teams). If already paying for SuperGrok Heavy/Ultra, effectively free on top; otherwise a significant jump. Who is this for? Not for people with a $20-50/month AI budget or still figuring out how AI helps them — start with Claude Code/Codex or cheaper tools first. Yes for power users already spending on multiple subscriptions who want to eliminate infrastructure overhead. It does not do anything fundamentally new — it removes setup/maintenance headache. Convenience + persistence + multi-Bot coordination is what makes it superior for serious users. Current limitations (beta): high price, data lives in SpaceXAI/Cursor cloud, shared computer per account (all Bots see the same logins/files), still early beta.

📝 要約

🎯 SpaceXAI（旧xAI）とCursorが共同開発した常時稼働型AIエージェント「Grok Bot」の実践ガイド。専用の永続クラウドPC（Linux VM）を持つ複数のBotが自律的にツールにログインして作業を完遂する仕組みと、セットアップ手順・実運用例・料金（月200〜300ドル）を踏まえた「誰に向いているか」を率直に評価する記事。

📌 ポイント

「チャットは閉じたら死ぬがBotは動き続ける」

通常のチャットセッションはウィンドウを閉じると終わるが、Grok Botは永続クラウドPCで動き続ける。複数Botが同一コンピュータを共有し協調する「スウォーム」設計。

プラグアンドプレイ vs データ主権

OpenClaw/Hermesは強力だが自前インフラの構築・維持が必要。Grok Botはダウンロード・ログインだけで完結する代わりに、データはSpaceXAI/Cursorのクラウド上にある（クローズド・データ主権とのトレードオフ）。

誰に向いているかの率直な線引き

月20〜50ドル予算の人やAI活用を模索中の人には不向き（Claude Code/Codexなど安価なツールから始めるべき）。複数サブスクに既に投資している重度AIユーザーには、インフラ運用の手間を消す価値がある、という明確な対象読者の切り分け。

💡 Creative Direction Note

ゆりこさんへの活用提案

① 「タスク構造化」の型がそのまま流用できる Botへの指示を「outcome + sources + constraints + deliverable + review point」で構造化する型は、CORINへの依頼の仕方・タスクMDの「次の一歩」の書き方にも応用できる。

② 常時稼働エージェントの動向として社内AI推進の参考に 「チャットは閉じると死ぬがBotは動き続ける」という対比は、nightly-vault-buildのような夜間バッチの価値を社内に説明する時のキャッチーな比喩になる。
