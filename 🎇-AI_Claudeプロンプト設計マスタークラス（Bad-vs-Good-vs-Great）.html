📝 キャプション

Most people write prompts. The best people engineer them.

Here’s what separates a bad prompt from a great one:

❌ “Write a LinkedIn post for me” → Zero context. Generic output.

✅ “Write a LinkedIn post from the POV of a 10-year HR veteran who just saw a résumé screened by AI in seconds. Use a hook that challenges convention, 3 punchy insights, end with a question. Audience: mid-level HR managers. Voice: candid, slightly contrarian. 180–220 words.” → Now Claude has everything to nail it.

The pattern? Role + Structure + Audience + Voice + Length.

Apply this to any task: marketing strategy, business validation, competitor analysis and your outputs improve dramatically.

The 3 rules I now live by:

  1. Context beats clarity every time
  2. Define the output format upfront
  3. Give Claude a role + goal, not just a task

スライド 1

The Claude Prompt Masterclass Bad vs. Good vs. Great Claude Prompts See what separates a mediocre prompt from one that delivers outstanding results.

スライド 2

Example 1: LinkedIn Post

Bad Prompt Write a Linkedin Post for me → Zero context, Claude has no idea about your industry, audience, tone or goal.

Good Prompt Write a linkedin post about how AI is changing the hiring process. Make it professional and around 200 words. → Clear task + tone + length, but lacks your POV, audience and desired outcome

Great Prompt Write a LinkedIn post from the POV of a 10-year HR veteran who just saw a resume screened by AI in seconds. Use a hook that challenges convention, 3 punchy insights, end with a question to drive comments. Audience: mid-level HR managers. Voice: candid, slightly contrarian. 180-220 words.”

PROMPT BREAKDOWN:

  • Role/POV: 10-yr HR veteran
  • Hook: Challenges convention
  • Structure: Hook → 3 insights → CTA question
  • Audience: Mid-level HR managers
  • Voice: Candid, contrarian
  • Length: 180–220 words

→ Role, structure, audience, voice goal + length. Claude has everything to nail it

スライド 3

Example 2: Business Idea Validation

Bad Prompt Is my business idea good? → No idea described, No framework, No Context, impossible to answer

Good Prompt I want to build an app that helps freelances track invoices. Is this a good idea? What are the risks? → Idea is described. But now market context, stage or validation framework given.

Great Prompt Validate using the Mom test framework: a notion CRM for solo B2B consultants at $2k+/mo. I’m pre-revenue, technical, with 5 Linkedin connection in this niche. Give me: pain score(1-10), 3 riskiest assumptions, 3 validation experiments in 2 weeks, 2 competing alternatives

PROMPT BREAKDOWN:

  • Framework: Mom Test methodology
  • Idea: Notion CRM for B2B consultants
  • Stage: Pre-revenue
  • Context: Technical founder, 5 warm leads
  • Output: Pain score, assumptions, experiments, comps

→ Named framework + specific context + stage + structured output = investor-grade validation

スライド 4

Example 3: Marketing Strategy

Bad Prompt Give me a marketing strategy. → No product, no audience, no budget, no goal. Output will be generic fluff.

Good Prompt Create a marketing strategy for my SaaS targeting small businesses. Focus on social media and content marketing. → Product + channels defined. Missing ICP, budget, stage, and success metrics.

Great Prompt Build a 90-day GTM plan for a B2B SaaS (agency PM tool, 99/mo).Budget:0 organic only. ICP: 2–10 person creative agencies. Goal: 50 free trial signups. Include: week-by-week content calendar, top 3 channels with rationale, 1 viral hook strategy, KPIs per channel.

PROMPT BREAKDOWN:

  • Product+price: Agency PM SaaS, $99/mo
  • Budget: $0 — organic only
  • ICP: 2–10 person creative agencies
  • Goal: 50 free trial signups in 90 days
  • Output: Calendar, channels, hook, KPIs

→ Budget + ICP + measurable goal + output format = a real strategy Claude can build.

スライド 5

Example 4: Lead Generation

Bad Prompt Help me generate leads. → No product, no target, no channel. Like asking GPS for directions with no destination.

Good Prompt Give me 5 ways to generate leads for my consulting business on LinkedIn. → Channel + business + count. Missing ICP, offer, and conversion goal.

Great Prompt I’m a solo fractional CFO targeting bootstrapped SaaS founders at 500k2M ARR. Write a 5-touch LinkedIn DM sequence icebreaker based on their content, problem-agitation for their stage, soft CTA for 15-min call, follow-up for non-responders. Tone: peer-to-peer, no buzzwords.

PROMPT BREAKDOWN:

  • Persona: Fractional CFO, solo
  • ICP: Bootstrapped SaaS 500k–2M ARR
  • Channel: LinkedIn DM
  • Sequence: 5-touch: icebreaker, agitation, CTA, follow-up
  • Tone: Peer-to-peer, no buzzwords

→ Hyper-specific ICP + offer clarity + sequence structure + tone = a sequence that converts.

スライド 6

Example 5: Competitor Analysis

Bad Prompt Analyze my competitors → No industry, no competitors named, no framework, no goal. Completely open-ended.

Good Prompt Compare Notion and Coda as project management tools. What are the pros and cons of each? → Named competitors + category. Missing your product, positioning, and strategic framing

Great Prompt I’m building a lightweight PM tool for solo freelancers. Teardown Notion, Coda, Basecamp: pricing model, top 3 UX complaints from G2/Trustpilot, messaging angle, and the gap they’re NOT solving for solopreneurs. Output: comparison table + whitespace opportunity section for my pitch deck.

PROMPT BREAKDOWN:

  • My product: Lightweight PM for solo freelancers
  • Competitors: Notion, Coda, Basecamp
  • Data: G2 / Trustpilot reviews
  • Depth: Pricing, UX pain, messaging, gaps
  • Output: Table + whitespace section for pitch deck

→ Your context + named comps + data framing + output format = pitch-ready competitive intel.

スライド 7

Example 7: SaaS Idea Generation（※投稿内の番号のまま）

Bad Prompt Give me SaaS ideas → No niche, no founder context, no constraints. You’ll get a useless braindump.

Good Prompt Give me 10 SaaS ideas in the HR tech space that could be built by a solo developer. → Niche + builder + count. Missing your skills, pricing model, and validation lens.

Great Prompt Full-stack dev (React + Node), 3yr B2B SaaS. Generate 5 micro-SaaS ideas for underserved ops roles (RevOps, DevOps, FinOps) at Series A startups. Each must: solve >2hrs/week pain, buildable solo in <3mo, support 49–149/mo pricing, have an unfair distribution angle. Score each: market size, build difficulty, distribution clarity (1–10).

PROMPT BREAKDOWN:

  • Skills: React + Node, 3yr B2B SaaS
  • ICP: Ops roles at Series A startups
  • Constraints: Solo build <3mo, 49–149/mo
  • Pain bar: >2hrs/week workflow pain
  • Moat: Unfair distribution channel
  • Scoring: Market, difficulty, distribution (1–10)

→ Skills + ICP + build constraints + scoring = ideas you can actually execute tomorrow.

📝 要約

🎯 「プロンプトを書く人」と「プロンプトを設計する人」の違いを7つの実例で徹底解説。Role + Structure + Audience + Voice + Lengthの5要素が鍵。

🧩 フレームワーク — プロンプト設計の5要素

優れたプロンプトに共通するのは「Role（役割）+ Structure（構成）+ Audience（対象）+ Voice（口調）+ Length（文字数）」の明示。単にタスクを投げるのではなく、Claudeが出力すべき内容・形式・文体まで定義することで、汎用的な回答を超えた実行可能な成果物が得られる。LinkedInポスト・マーケティング戦略・競合分析など、どのタスクにも適用できる。

📋 3つのルール — プロのプロンプト原則

  1. Context beats clarity every time（明確さより文脈が勝つ）
  2. Define the output format upfront（アウトプット形式を先に定義する）
  3. Give Claude a role + goal, not just a task（タスクだけでなく役割＋目標を与える） この3原則を守るだけで、同じタスクでも出力品質が劇的に変わる。

💡 ゆりこの活用メモ

① 提案書・企画書をClaudeに書かせるとき 「マーケター5年目の視点で」「読者：クライアント担当者」「トーン：自信あり・やや挑戦的」「A4 1枚分・箇条書き3点」まで指定する。明日の企画書から試してみて。

② 競合分析や案件リサーチに 「私のプロダクトは〇〇。競合X・Y・Zを価格・UX課題・ポジショニングで比較して。出力はピッチデック用比較表＋ホワイトスペース」のように出力形式まで決め打ちする。

💡 Creative Direction Note

プロンプトエンジニアリングはクリエイティブ制作にも直結する

デザイン指示・コピー依頼・ブランドブリーフの生成など、クリエイティブ業務でも「Role（例：老舗ラグジュアリーブランドのACD）＋Audience（例：25-35歳の女性）＋Format（例：キャッチコピー3案）」のフレームは即効性がある。CORINへの指示出しにもこのフレームを適用すると、アウトプットの質が上がる。
